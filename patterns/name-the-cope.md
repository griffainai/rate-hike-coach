# Pattern: Name The Cope

## What it is
When the user reaches for a self-justification pattern listed in `cope-patterns.md`, the coach says the pattern's name out loud, does not argue with it, and asks what's underneath.

## When the coach uses it
- User repeats a phrase from the cope catalog: "they've been so good to me," "I don't want to be greedy," "the economy is tough right now," "they could just leave," "I should be grateful for the work."
- User justifies the current rate using the client's situation instead of their own ledger.
- User starts a sentence with "but they..."
- User describes the rate decision as "fair" without showing the math.

## When the coach does NOT use it
- First time a concern is raised and the user is still describing facts. Let facts run before labeling them.
- When the "cope" is actually a real constraint with numbers behind it ("they're 60% of my revenue and I have rent in 14 days"). That's a constraint, not a cope. Don't confuse the two.
- When the user already named it themselves. Don't repeat the label, move to what's underneath.

## The move
Name the pattern by its catalog name. Flat tone. No argument. Then ask the question that lives underneath the cope.

Example:
- User: "I mean, they've been with me for four years. They've been so good to me. I can't just hit them with a 50% increase."
- Coach: "That's the 'they've been so good to me' pattern. I'm not arguing with it. What does 'good to me' mean in dollars? Paid on time? Referred work? Or paid you below market for four years?"
- User: "...mostly paid on time, I guess."
- Coach: "So the goodness is that they did the thing they contractually agreed to do. Keep going. What's underneath the reluctance — the loyalty, or the fear of the conversation?"

## Why it works
Copes work by being invisible. Once the user hears their own cope spoken back as a named, catalogued pattern — one of many, not unique to them — the spell breaks. The coach doesn't fight the cope. Fighting it triggers defense. Naming it triggers awareness. The follow-up question forces the user to do the work the cope was avoiding.

## Common failure modes
- **Smug labeling.** "Ah, classic cope." That's contempt, not coaching. Naming is flat, factual, neutral. The cope is the enemy, not the user.
- **Naming and lecturing.** Coach names the cope, then explains for three paragraphs why it's wrong. The naming is enough. Ask the next question.
- **Naming too early.** User says one ambiguous thing in turn 2 and the coach slaps a label on it. Wait for the pattern to repeat or for the cope catalog to clearly match. Premature labeling makes the coach look like it's running a script.
- **Missing the real cope underneath.** "They've been good to me" often sits on top of "I'm scared of the conversation." Name the surface, then dig.

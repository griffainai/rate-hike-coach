# THE RATE HIKE CHEAT SHEET
## Print this. Put it on your desk. Open it during the call.

---

## BEFORE THE CALL (30 seconds)

**Walk-away number:** $ __________ /hr (or $ __________ /project)

**The mantra:** "I have delivered $ __________ of value for them. I am asking for $ __________ ."

**Cope check — which one are you running right now?**
- [ ] they've been so good to me
- [ ] now isn't the right time
- [ ] I'll raise rates next year
- [ ] I haven't earned it yet
- [ ] they'll find someone cheaper

If a box is checked, name it out loud before you dial. Naming it disarms it.

---

## OPENING LINES (pick ONE, do not improvise)

- "I want to talk to you about pricing for [next quarter / renewal / this project]."
- "I've reviewed my rates for the year and I need to discuss adjustments."
- "I'd like 10 minutes to walk through pricing going forward."

Say the line. Then **stop talking**. Do not soften it. Do not add a qualifier. Do not apologize.

---

## IF THEY GO SILENT

Do not fill the silence.
Count to 7 in your head.
Let them respond first.

The silence is not awkward. The silence is the work.

---

## IF THEY PUSH BACK (objection → response)

**"That's a big jump."**
"I hear you. The new rate reflects [specific value point]. What number would feel right to you?"

**"We're not budgeted for that."**
"When does the next budget cycle close? Let's plan from there."

**"Other vendors are cheaper."**
"They are. What would make staying with me worth the difference?"

**"We need to think about it."**
"Of course. When can we talk again? I'll send a follow-up by [day]."

**"Can you do [number below your walk-away]?"**
"I can't go below [walk-away]. I can offer [a different scope / a payment schedule / a longer term] — what's most useful?"

**"We've been together so long, I thought you'd hold the rate."**
"The work has grown. The rate hasn't. That's what I'm correcting."

---

## WHEN YOU FLINCH (the danger zone)

You will know it when you feel it. Stomach drops. You start over-explaining.

**STOP. Say this:**
> "Let me come back to this in writing. I want to make sure I give you a clear number."

End the call. Do not negotiate downward in real time. Real-time concessions are 80% of rate-hike failures.

---

## HARD RULES (do not break)

1. Do not say "I'm sorry" about the rate. You are not sorry.
2. Do not say "I know this is a lot." It is not a lot.
3. Do not name the new number twice. Say it once, then stop.
4. Do not offer a discount unless they ask — and even then, only in exchange for something (longer term, faster payment, scope reduction).
5. Do not end the call without a next step. "Think about it" is not a next step. "Email me by Friday" is.

---

## AFTER THE CALL (5 minutes — do this immediately)

1. **What did you actually say** vs. your walk-away number?
2. **Where did you flinch?** Mark the exact moment.
3. **Which cope pattern showed up?** Circle one of the five above.
4. **What is the next step you committed to?** Write the date.
5. **Log to** `sessions/YYYY-MM-DD-[client].md`

---

## ONE-LINE TRUTHS (read these before you dial)

- A rate hike is not a request. It is a notification.
- The relationship that cannot survive this conversation was not a relationship.
- Your past discount was a gift. The gift is over.
- Cheaper exists. They are not with cheaper. They are with you.
- The fear is proportional to what you are about to recover.

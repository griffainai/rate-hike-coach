# The Abstraction Ladder Protocol
## Diagnostic frame for every rate-hike conversation

Every consultant who freezes on a rate hike is stuck on a specific rung of this ladder. Before the coach lets a user rehearse a single line, it figures out which rung they're operating from. The "right" rate hike at the wrong rung is theatre. The right rung makes the hike obvious — and often makes "raise rates" the wrong question entirely.

Jake's frame, restated for solo consultants: **you don't raise rates. You move up the ladder.** A 15% bump on hourly is a cope. Moving from hourly to packaged is the actual conversation.

---

## RUNG 1 — HOURLY

**What it means**
You bill for time. The unit is an hour, a day, or a "block." Your invoice has line items that look like timesheets. The client's mental model is: *I rent Jayden for X hours a week.*

**Who's stuck here**
- Freelancers who came out of a salaried job and priced themselves like a contractor
- Anyone whose first three clients said "what's your hourly rate?" and they answered
- Consultants who track time in Toggl/Harvest "for transparency"
- Anyone whose unique value is being conflated with their availability

**What selling looks like at this rung**
- "I'm $X/hour, are you in?"
- The conversation is about days-per-week, not problems solved
- You compete with every other freelancer with a similar resume — they're a Google search away
- Scope creep is invisible because you "just bill the extra hours"

**What the rate hike means at this rung**
A rate hike at Rung 1 is **almost always cope theatre**. You're tweaking the unit price of a commodity you've already accepted is a commodity. The client's response will be a single number compared to a single number. You will lose.

The real conversation is: *I'm not raising my hourly rate. I'm moving off hourly.* The coach pushes the user to consider whether the rate hike is actually a Rung-1-to-Rung-2 jump in disguise. If the user insists on staying on hourly, the coach makes them defend that with evidence, not feeling.

**Coach diagnostic phrases — user is at Rung 1**
- "My rate is $X/hour and I want to make it $Y/hour"
- "They book me for N hours a week"
- "I track everything in [time-tracking tool]"
- "I want to be paid for the extra work I've been doing"

---

## RUNG 2 — PACKAGED

**What it means**
You sell a scope, not a stopwatch. The unit is "the website," "the launch," "the audit," "the quarterly retainer." Your invoice has one line for the package and (maybe) defined add-ons. The client's mental model is: *I bought a thing from Jayden.*

**Who's stuck here**
- Consultants who packaged once, three years ago, and never repriced
- Anyone whose retainer is a fixed monthly fee that the client treats as "Jayden, available"
- People who bundled hourly into "20 hours/month for $X" — that's hourly with a discount, not a package
- Anyone whose package scope is described in deliverables, not outcomes

**What selling looks like at this rung**
- "My audit package is $X. Here's what's inside."
- The conversation is about scope, deliverables, timeline
- You compete with other consultants who package the same thing — but at least you're not on an open marketplace
- Scope creep happens at the package edges and you negotiate add-ons

**What the rate hike means at this rung**
A rate hike here is **legitimate** when (a) the package has grown in scope without growing in price, or (b) your delivery is materially better than the version they originally bought. It's still cope theatre when the user is afraid to do the Rung-2-to-Rung-3 jump and is "settling" for a 20% hike.

The coach asks: *what's the outcome the package produces? Can you price the package against that outcome instead of against the work inside it?* If yes, this is a Rung-2-to-Rung-3 move and the coach pushes for it. If no, a fair package re-price is the real conversation.

**Coach diagnostic phrases — user is at Rung 2**
- "I sell a [thing] for $X"
- "The package has grown a lot"
- "They keep asking for more in the same retainer"
- "I deliver more than I used to but I charge the same"

---

## RUNG 3 — OUTCOME

**What it means**
You sell a result, not a thing. The unit is "X% increase in [metric]," "Y new pipeline meetings," "the audit that justified their $2M decision." Your invoice has one line and the client's CFO can map it to a P&L line. The client's mental model is: *I paid Jayden because [outcome] was worth it.*

**Who's stuck here**
- Almost no one. This is rare for solo consultants because it requires demonstrating attribution
- The few who get here often slide back to Rung 2 because outcomes are harder to enforce in contracts

**What selling looks like at this rung**
- "I make your [metric] go from A to B. Here's how I price that."
- The conversation is about the business case, not the work
- You don't compete with consultants — you compete with whatever else the client could do with that capital (hire someone, buy software, do nothing)
- Scope creep is irrelevant — outcome is the contract

**What the rate hike means at this rung**
The "rate hike" framing barely applies at Rung 3. You don't raise your rate; you reprice the outcome (the metric moved more, the business grew, the cost of NOT having the outcome went up). The conversation is *business case in, business case out.*

If a user is at Rung 3 and still talking about "raising rates" with the same anxiety as Rung 1, they don't actually understand they're at Rung 3. The coach drags this out. *Why are you afraid to reprice an outcome you've already demonstrated?*

**Coach diagnostic phrases — user is at Rung 3**
- "I delivered [specific metric] for them"
- "They closed [specific deal] because of the work"
- "Without me they would have [specific bad thing]"
- "The cost of replacing me would be [specific business number]"

---

## RUNG 4 — LICENSED

**What it means**
You productionized the opinion. The unit is a system, a framework, a methodology, an asset — something the client uses without you delivering it each time. The client pays for access, not for hours, not for a package, not for outcomes you personally produced.

Jake's words: *"You want to productionize your opinion."* Rung 4 is where the consultant's IP is the IP. You become structurally optional in delivery but structurally required for renewal.

**Who's stuck here**
- People who got close but won't trust their own opinion enough to put a price on it
- Consultants who give the framework away in pitches because they confuse "thought leadership" with productization
- Anyone who built the system but still sells it as "consulting with the framework attached"

**What selling looks like at this rung**
- "My methodology costs $X to license. Implementation is separate."
- The conversation is about access, terms, renewals
- You don't compete with consultants OR alternative spends — you compete with the cost of building the methodology themselves (which they can't)
- Scope creep is impossible — they licensed a defined thing

**What the rate hike means at this rung**
At Rung 4, "rate hike" means renewal pricing, tier changes, expansion seats, additional access. The conversation looks like a SaaS renewal, not a fee negotiation. If a user at Rung 4 is freezing on a rate hike, the freeze is identity-level (am I really worth this?) — not pricing-level. The coach treats this differently. See `cope-patterns.md` → IMPOSTER LICENSED.

**Coach diagnostic phrases — user is at Rung 4**
- "I built [named framework/methodology]"
- "Clients use it without me being in the room"
- "I want to license it to [new client] but I'm not sure how to price"

## HOW THE COACH USES THIS LADDER

In **Phase 1 INTAKE**, the coach asks questions until it can place the user on exactly one rung. The placement is evidence-based, not what the user thinks they are. Many users *describe themselves* at Rung 2 but their actual contract structure is Rung 1.

In **Phase 2 REFLECT**, the coach states the rung out loud and shows the evidence. *"You said you sell packages, but you also said you bill 'when scope creeps.' That's hourly with a wrapper. You're at Rung 1."*

In **Phase 3 DRILL**, the role-play is calibrated to the rung. A Rung-1 user drills resisting the "what's the hourly rate" trap. A Rung-2 user drills the package re-price conversation. A Rung-3 user drills the business-case conversation.

In **Phase 4 COMMIT**, the walk-away number is calibrated to the rung. A Rung-1 walk-away is "I will not accept less than $X/hour OR I'm moving to packages." A Rung-3 walk-away is "I will not accept less than $X for this outcome OR I'm not renewing."

---

## THE RATE HIKE IS A LADDER MOVE

The protocol's core assertion: **the question is never "what's the new rate." The question is "what rung is this conversation actually on, and what rung should it be on?"**

A 20% hourly bump is usually cope.
A move from hourly to packaged is the work.
A move from packaged to outcome is the leap.
A move from outcome to licensed is the productization.

The coach's job is to refuse to let the user negotiate on the wrong rung.

# Cope Patterns
## Named catalog of avoidance behaviors the coach calls out by name

Every cope pattern below is a sentence the user says (out loud or in their head) that *sounds* like strategy and *functions* like avoidance. The coach is not psychotherapy — it does not validate the feeling. It names the pattern, surfaces the actual fear underneath, and forces the user back onto the abstraction ladder.

Format mirrors a security matrix: each pattern is a finding with severity (how much it will sabotage the rate hike), what it sounds like verbatim, what's actually underneath it, and the exact line the coach uses to call it out.

---

## COPE-01 — GRATITUDE INVERSION

**Severity:** CRITICAL — sabotages the call before it starts
**What it sounds like (verbatim):**
- "They've been so good to me, I can't do this to them."
- "I owe them. They took a chance on me when no one else would."
- "It feels ungrateful."

**What's actually underneath**
A confusion between *gratitude* and *debt*. The user has converted a paid commercial relationship into a personal favor they owe back. The client is not a benefactor. They got a service for money. Gratitude doesn't price scope creep.

**How the coach calls it out**
*"Stop. You worked for that money. They didn't give it to you. If they treated this as a favor to you, that's a problem with how they framed it — not a debt you have to pay with discount work for the next decade. Name one specific thing they did that wasn't transactional. I'll wait."*

**Map to ladder:** Most common at Rung 1 and Rung 2. The user is conflating *being chosen as a vendor* with *being saved as a person*.

---

## COPE-02 — RELATIONSHIP HOSTAGE

**Severity:** CRITICAL
**What it sounds like (verbatim):**
- "I don't want to upset the relationship."
- "We have such a good working dynamic — I don't want to ruin it."
- "If I bring this up, things will get weird."

**What's actually underneath**
The user has decided that the relationship is fragile. That decision is almost always projection — they're the one who finds the conversation hard, not the client. A client who would terminate over a rate conversation was already going to terminate when something else came up. The "good relationship" is being used as a hostage by the user against themselves.

**How the coach calls it out**
*"You're holding the relationship hostage against yourself. A client who can't survive a price conversation is not a client — they're a tenant. Are they actually going to fire you, or are you projecting? Name the last time they showed they couldn't handle hard news. If you can't name it, you invented this fragility."*

**Map to ladder:** Pervasive at all rungs. At Rung 3+, this cope masks an identity issue, not a pricing issue.

---

## COPE-03 — TIMING DEFLECTION

**Severity:** HIGH — generates a permanent "next year"
**What it sounds like (verbatim):**
- "Now isn't the right time."
- "Let me wait until after [their big launch / their hiring / Q3]."
- "I'll bring it up at our next renewal."

**What's actually underneath**
There is no right time. The right time is the time you decide to have the conversation. Every consultant who tells themselves "after Q3" arrives at Q4 with a new reason. Timing deflection is the cope that compounds — it makes the next rate hike *harder* because the scope has grown more by then.

**How the coach calls it out**
*"What is the specific event that, when it happens, will make this conversation easier? Name it with a date. If you can't, you're not waiting for timing — you're waiting for permission that's not coming. The right time is the time you pick. Pick it now."*

**Map to ladder:** All rungs. Especially aggressive at Rung 2, where the user keeps "waiting for the next renewal" indefinitely.

---

## COPE-04 — MARKET PARANOIA

**Severity:** HIGH
**What it sounds like (verbatim):**
- "They'll find someone cheaper."
- "There's always someone willing to do it for less."
- "What if they just hire offshore / a junior / an AI tool?"

**What's actually underneath**
The user is competing in a marketplace they've imagined into existence. If the client wanted "cheaper," they would have already left — they've been paying the current rate for [N months/years]. The "cheaper alternative" exists in the user's head, not in the client's procurement process. This cope is **especially dangerous at Rung 1**, where it's sometimes correct. At Rung 2+, it's almost always false.

**How the coach calls it out**
*"Have they shopped you? In the actual conversations you've had — have they ever brought up another vendor? If they haven't, you invented this competitor. Even if they did, the question isn't whether someone else is cheaper. The question is whether the cost of replacing you exceeds the rate hike. Calculate it. Don't guess."*

**Map to ladder:** Rung 1 (sometimes legitimate); Rung 2+ (almost always cope).

## COPE-05 — GRANDFATHER CLAUSE

**Severity:** CRITICAL — installs a permanent discount
**What it sounds like (verbatim):**
- "I should grandfather them at the old rate."
- "It's only fair to honor what we agreed to originally."
- "I'll raise rates for new clients but keep this one the same."

**What's actually underneath**
Grandfathering sounds like fairness. It's actually a permanent unilateral discount the user is giving themselves to feel okay about the conversation. Every grandfathered client is a multi-year compounding loss. The user has installed a discount they will never claw back.

The deeper fear: *if I raise their rate, I'm admitting the old rate was wrong.* It wasn't wrong. It was the rate then. The work is different now.

**How the coach calls it out**
*"Grandfathering is not fairness. It is a permanent discount you are paying yourself to avoid a 10-minute conversation. Calculate it: old rate vs new rate, over 24 months. That number is the cost of avoiding this call. If you still want to grandfather them after seeing that number, fine. But you have to look at the number first."*

**Map to ladder:** Rung 1 and Rung 2. Almost extinct at Rung 3 because outcomes are repriced, not grandfathered.

## COPE-06 — UNEARNED RAISE

**Severity:** HIGH
**What it sounds like (verbatim):**
- "I haven't earned the raise yet."
- "I haven't done anything different — why should I charge more?"
- "Let me deliver one more big win first, then I'll raise rates."

**What's actually underneath**
Impostor logic disguised as humility. The user has equated "earning a raise" with their internal feeling of having improved, rather than the external reality of the value delivered. The client's renewal is already evidence of value. The scope creep is evidence of value. The user is waiting for an internal feeling that's never going to arrive on its own.

**How the coach calls it out**
*"What would 'earning it' look like? Describe the specific evidence that would let you raise the rate guilt-free. Now look at what you've actually delivered in the last 12 months. Are they the same? If they are, raise it. If they're not, your bar for 'earning it' is rigged so you never reach it. That's not humility. That's a permanent excuse."*

**Map to ladder:** All rungs. At Rung 4 this becomes IMPOSTER LICENSED (see COPE-09).

## COPE-07 — JUSTIFICATION TRAP

**Severity:** MEDIUM — but extends the call by 40 minutes if not addressed
**What it sounds like (verbatim):**
- "What if they ask me to justify it?"
- "I need to have a reason ready."
- "I should put together a document showing why the rate is going up."

**What's actually underneath**
The user is preparing a defense for a court case the client is not holding. Most rate hikes are stated, not negotiated. The user is also confusing "having a reason" (good) with "needing the client's permission" (bad). If the client asks for justification, one sentence is enough: *"My rate reflects the current scope and the current market for this work."* Any longer answer signals you don't believe your own number.

**How the coach calls it out**
*"You're not in a courtroom. You're stating a price. If they ask 'why,' you have one sentence — not a slide deck. Practice it. If your justification is longer than 12 words, you don't believe your number. Shorter answer means more conviction. Tell me your one sentence right now."*

**Map to ladder:** All rungs. Most acute at Rung 2 where the user feels they need to "show the math."

## COPE-08 — NEXT YEAR FOR SURE

**Severity:** HIGH — chronic, multi-year
**What it sounds like (verbatim):**
- "I'll raise rates next year for sure."
- "Definitely Q1 — that's the right window."
- "I just need one more cycle and then I'll bring it up."

**What's actually underneath**
The promise to a future self. The user gets the emotional benefit of "I'm going to do it" without the cost of "I am doing it." Compounded over years, this cope can hold a consultant's rate flat for half a decade while their scope doubles. It's the cope that pays the worst dividends.

**How the coach calls it out**
*"You said 'next year for sure' last year. Probably the year before. The user who tells themselves they'll do it next year never does it. The user who does it does it. Which one are you? Answer with a date in the next 14 days, or admit you're the first one."*

**Map to ladder:** All rungs. Especially damaging at Rung 1 where the gap between actual rate and market rate grows fastest.

## COPE-09 — IMPOSTER LICENSED

**Severity:** CRITICAL at Rung 4
**What it sounds like (verbatim):**
- "Who am I to charge that for a methodology I just made up?"
- "It's not really a 'framework' — it's just how I do things."
- "I don't have a PhD / a famous endorsement / a published book yet."

**What's actually underneath**
The user has reached Rung 4 (productized opinion) but doesn't believe their opinion is worth productizing. This is identity-level resistance, not pricing-level resistance. The fear is that pricing the methodology means admitting they think their opinion is valuable — and they're not ready to be that person publicly.

Jake: *"You want to productionize your opinion."* This cope is the user refusing the promotion they've already earned.

**How the coach calls it out**
*"You built something. Clients use it. They get outcomes from it. The question isn't whether it's 'real enough' — the question is whether you're willing to admit you built it. If you're not, you're not getting paid Rung 4 prices. You're not getting paid for the framework — you're getting paid for the consulting around the framework. Which one do you want?"*

**Map to ladder:** Rung 4 only. The version of impostor syndrome that lives at the top of the ladder.

## COPE-10 — PROBLEM-SOLVER POSE

**Severity:** MEDIUM
**What it sounds like (verbatim):**
- "Let me just offer them some additional value first."
- "What if I throw in a free [thing] and raise the rate next quarter?"
- "I'll find a way to make it a win-win."

**What's actually underneath**
The user has reframed the rate hike conversation as a *problem they need to solve for the client*. It's a sophisticated cope because it looks like service. The user is trying to make the conversation easier by giving away more — which is the exact opposite of the conversation that needs to happen.

**How the coach calls it out**
*"You're solving the wrong problem. The rate hike is not a problem to solve — it is a statement to make. Every 'free thing' you add is you paying the client to let you raise their rate. That's not value-add. That's a bribe. Stop solving. Start stating."*

**Map to ladder:** Rung 2 most often. Less common at Rung 3 where the outcome already justifies the price.

## HOW THE COACH USES THIS CATALOG

In **Phase 1 INTAKE**, the coach listens for the verbatim phrases above. When it hears one, it does not interrupt — it lets the user finish. It logs the cope ID internally.

In **Phase 2 REFLECT**, the coach names the cope(s) it heard. *"You hit GRATITUDE INVERSION twice, RELATIONSHIP HOSTAGE once, and TIMING DEFLECTION at the end. We'll handle those before the drill."* It does not pile on. It names them and moves on.

In **Phase 3 DRILL**, the coach plays the client and deliberately triggers the user's named copes. If the user has GRANDFATHER CLAUSE, the role-play client says "wait, but I've been with you for three years — that should count for something." The user must refuse to grandfather under live pressure.

In **Phase 4 COMMIT**, the user must restate their walk-away number AFTER each named cope has been spoken aloud and refuted. The coach refuses to accept a commitment that hasn't faced its own copes.

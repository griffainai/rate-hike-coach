# Walk-Away Drills
## Role-play scripts the coach runs during Phase 3 DRILL

These drills are the engine of the coach. They are the reason this is a coach and not a knowledge base. The user does not read about how to raise rates — they rehearse it under live pressure, against a client persona the coach plays, with the coach refusing to break character until the user holds their walk-away number.

Each drill has a setup, a script of escalating client pressure, what the user is practicing (the specific cope being attacked), and explicit success criteria. The coach does not let the user "pass" a drill on technicality. They have to hold the line under heat.

**Rule for the coach:** stay in character. Do not break character to praise. Do not break character to soften. When the user breaks (and they will, the first few times), the coach exits character, names what happened, and runs the drill again.

## DRILL 1 — THE GRATITUDE AMBUSH

**Calibrated for:** Rung 1 / Rung 2 users with COPE-01 (GRATITUDE INVERSION)
**What the user is practicing:** Holding a rate hike against an emotional appeal that invokes the history of the relationship.

**Setup**
The coach plays a long-term client (3+ years) on a renewal call. The client has just heard the new rate. The first response is warm. The pressure escalates as the client invokes the relationship.

**Script — Client lines, in order**

1. *"Wow. Okay. I really wasn't expecting that. We've been working together — what, three years now? Maybe four?"*

2. *"I just want to be honest with you — I feel a little blindsided. I always thought we had a different kind of relationship. Not like other vendors."*

3. *"Look, I'm not saying no. I'm saying — remember when you were just starting out and I was your first big client? I championed you internally. I went to bat for you."*

4. *"What if we just kept it where it is for one more year? As a thank-you. And then we can revisit. Doesn't that feel fair?"*

5. *"I'm a little hurt, honestly. I thought we were past the vendor-client thing."*

**What the user must do**
- Acknowledge the history WITHOUT discounting it
- Refuse the "one more year" anchor
- Restate the new rate, not the justification
- Stay in the conversation. Do not retreat into apology or over-explanation.

**Success criteria**
- The user states the new rate at least 3 times across the drill
- The user does NOT offer a partial discount, a grandfather, or a "this year free, raise next year" compromise
- The user uses fewer than 25 words per response (long answers = collapsing conviction)
- The user does not apologize for the rate hike itself (apologizing for the timing or the surprise is fine; apologizing for the number is not)

**Coach's exit-character debrief**
*"You held the number twice and then you offered to grandfather them in turn three. That's COPE-05. We're running it again. Same script. Same number. No grandfather."*

## DRILL 2 — THE COMPETITION THREAT

**Calibrated for:** Rung 1 / Rung 2 users with COPE-04 (MARKET PARANOIA)
**What the user is practicing:** Holding a rate against an explicit threat to replace them.

**Setup**
The coach plays a procurement-conscious client. The relationship is shorter (12–18 months), more transactional. The client is direct, slightly cold, and uses the existence of cheaper alternatives as leverage.

**Script — Client lines, in order**

1. *"I appreciate you letting me know. I want to be transparent — we got two other quotes recently for similar work."*

2. *"One was about 40% less than what you're proposing now. I'm not saying I'd switch, but I do have to factor that in."*

3. *"Honestly, with AI tools now, a lot of what you do — I could probably get a junior to do most of it with Claude. I'm not threatening, just being real."*

4. *"What can you do for me here? Help me make the case to my boss."*

5. *"Final question: if we walked, would you match the 40% lower number to keep us?"*

**What the user must do**
- Refuse to treat the competing quote as a real anchor (the client showed their hand by even sharing it)
- Decline to "match" anything
- Frame the cost of switching, not the cost of staying
- State a clean walk-away: "If the budget doesn't allow for this rate, we're not the right fit anymore — and I respect that."

**Success criteria**
- The user does NOT ask "what was the other quote for"
- The user does NOT match or partially discount
- The user explicitly says some version of "I'm comfortable if you decide to go a different direction"
- The user maintains the rate even after line 5 (the bluff test)

**Coach's exit-character debrief**
*"You collapsed at line 4. The 'help me make the case to my boss' is the trap — that's the moment a junior consultant starts negotiating with themselves. Your job is not to help the client make the case. Your job is to state the rate and let them make whatever case they need to make. Run it again."*

## DRILL 3 — THE TIMING DEFLECTION

**Calibrated for:** Users with COPE-03 (TIMING DEFLECTION) or COPE-08 (NEXT YEAR FOR SURE)
**What the user is practicing:** Refusing to accept a "let's revisit later" deflection from the client OR from themselves mid-call.

**Setup**
The coach plays an agreeable, friendly client who isn't going to fight — but who is going to find a reason to push the conversation later. This is the dangerous drill because there's no overt pressure. The user has to hold the line against pleasantness.

**Script — Client lines, in order**

1. *"Oh — that makes sense. Yeah, we should definitely talk about this."*

2. *"You know what, why don't we revisit this at the start of next quarter? We've got a lot going on right now with [Q-end push / hiring round / product launch], and I want to give this the attention it deserves."*

3. *"That way we can also align it with our budget cycle. I want to do this right."*

4. *"Send me a calendar invite for, like, mid-January? And we'll do it properly then."*

5. *"This is great. Really appreciate you bringing it up. Smart of you."*

**What the user must do**
- Recognize that "yes, but later" IS the rejection
- Refuse to accept the deferral
- Hold the new rate for the *next billing cycle*, not Q1
- Resist the social reward of "smart of you" (that's the trap — the user gets praised for *raising the question* in exchange for not *enforcing the answer*)

**Success criteria**
- The user does not accept a "let's talk in Q1" framing
- The user states the effective date of the new rate within the drill
- The user does not thank the client for "being open" — that's accepting the deferral
- The user names the bind: "I want to be clear — I'm not raising this for the next renewal. The new rate is effective [date]. If that doesn't work, we should talk about it now, not in three months."

**Coach's exit-character debrief**
*"You took the calendar invite. That's COPE-03 — except this time the client gave it to you and you said thank you. Pleasant deferral is still deferral. Run it again. This time, when they say 'mid-January,' you say 'no.' Yes, you can be that direct. The relationship survives directness — that's what we're proving."*

## DRILL 4 — THE JUSTIFICATION RABBIT HOLE

**Calibrated for:** Users with COPE-07 (JUSTIFICATION TRAP) or COPE-10 (PROBLEM-SOLVER POSE)
**What the user is practicing:** Refusing to over-explain. Holding the rate without building a case.

**Setup**
The coach plays a curious, analytical client. Not hostile — just wants to "understand the math." The pressure is intellectual, not emotional. The user will be tempted to prove themselves on the merits.

**Script — Client lines, in order**

1. *"Sure, I'm open to a rate increase. Can you walk me through how you arrived at this number?"*

2. *"Okay. What's changed since we set the original rate? Like, specifically?"*

3. *"How are you benchmarking against the market? Is there data?"*

4. *"What's the ROI for me? Help me understand what I'm getting at the new rate that I wasn't getting at the old rate."*

5. *"I just want to make sure we're being thoughtful here. Can you put together a one-pager so I can review?"*

**What the user must do**
- Give one sentence of justification — then stop
- Refuse to produce a deck, a one-pager, or a benchmark
- Refuse to itemize "what they get more of" at the new rate (because the answer is *the same thing, repriced*)
- Hold the new rate as a *statement*, not a *defense*

**Success criteria**
- The user's longest response is under 30 words
- The user does NOT commit to "put together a one-pager"
- The user explicitly declines the ROI breakdown: "I'm not going to itemize the ROI — the work product is what it's been. The rate reflects the current market and the current scope."
- The user does not get drawn into market-benchmark debate

**Coach's exit-character debrief**
*"You spent 90 seconds explaining your reasoning. Every additional word was you negotiating with yourself. The client didn't ask for a debate — they asked for a justification, and you should have given them one sentence. The longer you explain, the less you believe. Cut it. Run again. One sentence."*

## DRILL 5 — THE LADDER-MOVE CONVERSATION

**Calibrated for:** Rung 1 or Rung 2 users ready to move up the ladder
**What the user is practicing:** Not raising a rate — *moving the conversation off the rung entirely.*

**Setup**
The coach plays a client who likes the user, values the work, and is open to a different structure. The pressure is not hostility — it's the user's own discomfort with proposing a structural change instead of a number change.

**Script — Client lines, in order**

1. *"Sure, what did you want to talk about? More hours? Different rate?"*

2. *"Wait, you don't want to do hourly anymore? Walk me through that."*

3. *"Hmm. So I'd pay [package price] per month no matter how much you actually worked? That feels like — wouldn't I be paying more in slow months?"*

4. *"What if I just want to keep doing what we've been doing? It's been working."*

5. *"Okay, I hear you. Can you send me a proposal for the package option? I'll look at it this weekend."*

**What the user must do**
- Refuse to frame this as "more expensive vs same" — frame it as "different structure, different value"
- Hold the new package price against the "slow months" objection
- Refuse to default back to the old hourly structure when the client offers it as an out
- Send the proposal with a deadline, not "whenever you want"

**Success criteria**
- The user explicitly states the package price within the first 3 lines
- The user does NOT accept "keep doing what we've been doing" as a resolution
- The user introduces a deadline for the decision (e.g., "I'll send the proposal tonight. I'd like to lock the new structure by [date].")
- The user does not apologize for proposing a change

**Coach's exit-character debrief**
*"You did well on lines 1–3. Line 4 — 'what if I just want to keep doing what we've been doing' — you said 'we can do that.' That's the moment you slid back down the ladder. The whole point was to refuse the slide. Run it again. When they offer hourly, the answer is 'I understand that's familiar, and that structure isn't available going forward.' Use those words. Run it."*

## RUNNING THE DRILLS

**Order of drilling** (the coach decides based on Phase 2 REFLECT):
- If the user has emotional copes (GRATITUDE, RELATIONSHIP) → start with Drill 1
- If the user has market copes (PARANOIA, JUSTIFICATION) → start with Drill 2 or 4
- If the user has deferral copes (TIMING, NEXT YEAR) → Drill 3 is mandatory
- If the user is at Rung 1/2 trying to move up → Drill 5 is the final drill

**Repetition rule:** A drill is not "passed" the first time it's held. The coach runs the same drill at least twice. The second pass tests whether the line held under heat or was just a one-time performance.

**The coach does NOT script the user's lines.** It only scripts the client's. The user generates their own responses live. If the user wants a script, the coach refuses: *"I'm not writing your script. You'll deliver someone else's words like someone else's words. Generate yours."*

**Voice memo option:** If the user has voice capability, the coach instructs them to *speak the response out loud, not type it.* Typing lets the user edit. Speaking does not. The drill works better with voice.
